# Precedents — situations where the thesis resolved

A reference catalogue of *past cases* where high-ownership / deep-value /
structural-discount setups actually played out: delistings, tender offers,
minority squeeze-outs, management buyouts, and forced conversions. Use these as
base rates and templates when evaluating current candidates on the shortlist.

> Approximate figures, compiled from public reporting. Not investment advice.
> Verify terms and dates against primary filings before relying on them.

---

## 1. India — MNC / promoter delistings & squeeze-outs

The recurring pattern: a foreign parent near the 75% free-float ceiling makes a
delisting offer (reverse book-build) or squeezes out minorities.

| Company | Parent | Event | Terms / outcome |
|---------|--------|-------|-----------------|
| **Atlas Copco India** | Atlas Copco (SE) | Delisted 2011–12 | Parent held ~83.8%, bought out remaining ~16.2% |
| **Alfa Laval India** | Alfa Laval (SE) | Delisted 2012 | Reverse book-build delisting |
| **Carrier Aircon India** | Carrier / UTC (US) | Delisted (2000s) | Open offer + Sec. 100 squeeze-out |
| **Otis Elevator India** | UTC (US) | Delisted (2000s) | Minority squeeze-out |
| **Cadbury India** | Mondelez (US) | Squeeze-out ~2014 | Long court fight over "fair value" |
| **Hexaware Technologies** | Baring PE | Delisted 2020 | Exit price ₹475/sh; **re-listed 2025** |
| **GSK Consumer (Horlicks)** | Unilever | Merged into HUL 2020 | Share-swap, brand absorbed |
| **GlaxoSmithKline Pharma** | GSK (UK) | Open offer 2014 | Parent raised 50.7% → 75% (did *not* delist) |
| **Panasonic Appliances India** | Panasonic (JP) | Delisting proposal 2014 | Board approval |

**Read-through for the shortlist:** the Indian names that trade at *premium* P/B
(Honeywell Auto, 3M, Gillette, AstraZeneca) rarely delist cheaply — the catalyst
is the free-float rule, not value. The micro-caps near/over 75% with thin volume
(Sharp India, Fresenius Kabi Oncology) are the cleaner squeeze-out setups.

## 2. Japan — parent-subsidiary take-privates (Oyako-Jojo unwind)

TSE governance reform + activist pressure is dismantling parent-child listings.
Parents are buying subsidiaries out or selling them to PE.

## 3. Japan — MBOs & take-privates (deep value / cash-rich)

| Company | Sponsor | Year | Price / premium |
|---------|---------|------|-----------------|
| **Taisho Pharmaceutical** | Family MBO | 2023–24 | ¥8,620/sh, +50%, ~¥710bn; delisted Apr-2024 |
| **Benesse Holdings** | Mgmt + EQT | 2023–24 | ¥2,600/sh, +45%, ~¥208bn |
| **Outsourcing Inc.** | Bain Capital | 2023 | ~$1.53bn MBO |
| **Topcon** | KKR | 2025 | ¥3,300/sh, +99.5% vs 12-mo avg |
| **Shidax** | Family MBO | 2023–24 | ¥800/sh; court later ruled fair value ¥950 |
| **Nichii Gakkan** | Bain, then Nippon Life | 2020 | Contested (Baring counter-bid) |

**Read-through:** Japanese buyouts frequently print at large premiums *because*
the pre-bid price embedded the net-cash/low-P/B discount. Note the recurring
"price too light" litigation (Shidax, Taisho) — a live risk and an appraisal
opportunity.

## 6. Hong Kong — privatizations (scheme of arrangement)

- 2019–2020: **33 privatization offers**; most via scheme of arrangement (not
  general offer). Typical premium 30–50%.
- **Dickson Concepts (2025)** — Dickson Poon's take-private **FAILED** at the
  court meeting (10.16% opposed vs the 10% "headcount/veto" limit). A reminder
  that HK schemes can be blocked by a small dissenting minority.
- **Hang Seng Bank (2025)** — HSBC proposed privatization at HK$155/sh (+33%).
